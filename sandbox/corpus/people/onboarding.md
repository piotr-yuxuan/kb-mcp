Welcome to Quillmoor. This page walks you through your first two weeks: what happens
before you arrive, what to expect on day one, who will help you, and what you should
have done by the end of week two. Keep it open; most joiners come back to it several
times.

Your manager has their own version of this plan and will adapt it to your team. If
anything here does not match what happens to you, tell the People team: that is how
this page stays accurate.

*Owner: Rhiannon Dace, Head of people. Maintained by the People team. Last reviewed
13 July 2026.*

## Before your first day

About three weeks before you start, you receive an invitation to Haven, our HR
system, at your personal email address. Haven is where your contract, payslips and
leave live, so it is the first thing to set up.

In Haven, before day one:

1. Sign your contract and the confidentiality agreement, if you have not already.
2. Enter your bank details, emergency contact and home address.
3. Upload your right-to-work documents (UK) or your documents for the
   pre-employment declaration (France). The People team checks them and may ask to
   see the originals on day one.
4. Tell us about anything that would make your first days easier: a keyboard layout,
   an adjustable desk, a quiet room, a later start because of school runs. Nothing
   you share here goes to your team without your agreement.

At the same time, your manager raises your **JML joiner request**. That single
request creates your company identity, your email and chat accounts, your Haven
employee record and the standard access for your role. Access beyond the standard set
is requested separately once you have started (see "Accounts and access" below).

Your manager also chooses your **buddy** and books them for your first morning.

## Day one

### Where to go

- **London.** Come to Quill House reception at 09:30. Someone from the People team
  meets you there, gives you a temporary pass and takes you up to the welcome room.
  The [London office guide](../workplace/london-office.md) has directions, cycle
  parking and everything about the building.
- **Paris.** Come to the Maison Lanterne at 09:30 and ask for Élodie Vasseur, the
  Paris office manager, who will give you your badge. The practical guide is
  [Bureau de Paris : guide pratique](../workplace/bureau-de-paris.md).
- **Remote.** Join the welcome call at 09:30 UK time (10:30 in France). The link is in
  the calendar invitation sent to your personal email.

### Your laptop

Everyone receives their laptop on day one. In London and Paris it is handed over in
the welcome room, already enrolled in our device management, with disk encryption on.
If you work remotely, the laptop arrives by courier in a sealed box the working day
before; please do not open the box until the welcome call, where someone from IT
walks you through the first sign-in.

During that first sign-in you will:

- set your password and enrol your second factor, following the
  [Password and MFA standard](../security/password-and-mfa.md);
- receive a hardware security key if your role includes production administration;
- check the settings in the [Laptop security baseline](../security/laptop-baseline.md),
  which are already applied but worth understanding.

Bring nothing but yourself and your documents. Please do not sign into company
systems from a personal device; if you need a phone for chat, ask IT about a work
profile.

### Meeting your buddy

Your buddy is a colleague from another team who has been here at least a year. They
are not your manager and not your trainer: they are the person you can ask the
questions you might not want to ask anyone else. Where is the good coffee? Why is
everyone talking about Lantern? Is it fine to leave at 17:00?

Your buddy will:

- have coffee or lunch with you on day one;
- check in with you at least twice a week during your first two weeks;
- introduce you to two or three people outside your team;
- stay available afterwards, for as long as it is useful to you.

If the pairing does not work for any reason, ask the People team for another buddy.
Nobody needs to know why.

### The afternoon

Day one afternoons are deliberately light: you will meet your manager for an hour,
read this page and the pages it links to, and set up your chat profile with a photo
and your pronouns if you wish. Leave on time.

## Your first two weeks, day by day

Your manager's plan takes priority, but most joiners follow something close to this.

| When | What happens | With whom |
|---|---|---|
| Day 1 | Welcome, laptop, accounts, buddy coffee, first one-to-one | People team, IT, buddy, manager |
| Day 2 | Team introduction; read your team's pages and recent decisions | Your team |
| Day 3 | Security awareness and data protection training | Security, self-paced in Haven |
| Day 4 | Shadow a colleague for a day of real work | A teammate |
| Day 5 | End-of-week check-in: what is clear, what is not | Manager |
| Days 6 to 7 | A first small task with a clear finish line | Manager, teammate |
| Day 8 | Company induction session (if it falls in your fortnight; otherwise the next one) | Leadership team |
| Days 9 to 10 | Finish the first task; meet the teams you will work with most | Manager, buddy |
| Day 10 | Two-week review: agree your goals for the rest of probation | Manager |

The **company induction** runs on the first Tuesday of every month, in London with a
video link for Paris and remote colleagues. Hollis Varga, our chief executive, opens
it; Ottilie Marsh, Idris Kallenberg and Ama Rowntree explain the technology, the
numbers and the products. It is the best place to understand how card acceptance,
payouts, Merchant Hub and the Merchant API fit together.

## Accounts and access

Your accounts come from the JML process: the joiner request that your manager raised
before you arrived. Later, when you change team or role, your manager raises a JML
mover request, and when you leave, a leaver request. You never request your standard
access yourself.

Anything beyond the standard access for your role (a production system, a finance
tool, a customer data set) is requested through the access request form in the
service portal. Each request needs your manager's approval and the system owner's,
and every grant is checked again in the regular access reviews. How those reviews
work, and what they mean for you, is on
[Quarterly access reviews](../security/access-reviews.md).

A few rules that surprise new joiners:

- Ask only for the access you need now, not for what you might need later. It is
  quick to add access, and every review is quicker when there is less to certify.
- Never share an account, even for a minute.
- If you receive access you did not ask for, say so in the service portal.
- Report anything odd, such as an unexpected login prompt or an email asking for your
  password, using the "Report phish" button, as described in
  [Reported phishing](../security/phishing.md).

## Where you will work

Most teams mix office days and home days. Desks in our offices are booked in Perch,
and the rules for booking, team days and checking in are on
[Desk booking and hybrid working](../workplace/desk-booking.md). Your manager will
tell you which days your team usually meets in person; for your first two weeks, we
suggest coming in on those days if you can.

If you will need a particular desk or equipment, the People team arranges it with the
Workplace team before you arrive.

## Money matters

### Pay

You are paid monthly. Your first payslip appears in Haven a few days before payday;
the dates, and what happens if you join after the monthly cut-off, are on
[Payroll dates](payroll-dates.md).

### Expenses

If you spend your own money on work in your first weeks, for example a train ticket
to the office for your first day or a cable you were told to buy, keep the receipt.
Claims, limits and approvals are all in the [Expenses policy](../finance/expenses.md),
and French colleagues follow [Notes de frais (France)](../finance/notes-de-frais.md).
Read it before you book any business travel.

### Benefits

UK joiners are enrolled in the pension scheme and private medical insurance
automatically; you can change your choices in Haven during your first month. See
[Benefits in the UK](benefits-uk.md). French joiners are covered by the company's
mutuelle and prévoyance from their first day: see
[Mutuelle et prévoyance (France)](mutuelle-et-prevoyance.md).

## Leave and absence

You can book annual leave from your first week. If you already have a trip booked
when you join, tell your manager during your first week so that it can be approved in
Haven.

- UK contracts: [Annual leave policy (UK)](annual-leave-uk.md).
- French contracts: [Congés payés et RTT (France)](conges-payes-rtt.md).
- If you are unwell, the rules for telling your manager are on
  [Sickness absence](sickness-absence.md). They apply from day one.

## Training you must complete in your first two weeks

All of these are in Haven, under "My learning", and take about three hours in total.

| Course | Time | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Security awareness | 45 minutes | Passwords, phishing, devices, reporting |
| Data protection and GDPR | 40 minutes | How we handle personal data, and who to ask |
| Anti-bribery and gifts | 20 minutes | Our [anti-bribery policy](../legal/anti-bribery.md) in practice |
| Payments essentials | 60 minutes | Card payments, payouts and settlement, for everyone |
| Speaking up | 15 minutes | How to raise a concern, including [whistleblowing](../legal/whistleblowing.md) |

Engineers have an extra list, starting with their development environment and the
deployment pipeline: see the [New engineer checklist](../engineering/new-engineer-checklist.md).

## Your probation period

Your first months at Quillmoor are a probation period, during which you and your
manager meet regularly to check that the role is working for both of you. The
two-week review on day 10 is the first of those meetings. How probation works, and
what happens at the end of it, is on [Probation period](probation.md).

## A checklist for the end of week two

By the end of your tenth working day, you should be able to tick every line below. If
you cannot, it is almost always because something has not reached you yet; tell your
manager.

- I have signed my contract and my details in Haven are complete.
- My laptop is set up, my second factor is enrolled and I have locked my screen at
  least once on purpose.
- I have met my buddy at least three times.
- I know which days my team is in the office, and I have booked a desk in Perch.
- I have completed the training courses in Haven.
- I have requested any access I need beyond the standard set, and nothing more.
- I have read the annual leave policy for my country and booked any leave I had
  already planned.
- I have read the expenses policy before claiming anything.
- I have had my two-week review and have written goals for the rest of my
  probation.
- I know who to ask when I am stuck: my manager, my buddy, or the People team.

## For managers

Your part of onboarding starts before the joiner does.

1. Raise the JML joiner request as soon as the offer is accepted, and at the latest
   ten working days before the start date, so that the laptop and accounts are ready
   on day one.
2. Choose a buddy from another team and book their first-morning coffee.
3. Block an hour for a one-to-one on day one and half an hour at the end of each
   week.
4. Prepare a first task that can be finished within the two weeks. A small, real
   change is better than an exercise.
5. Hold the two-week review on day 10 and record the agreed goals in Haven.

If the start date moves, update the JML request on the same day: a laptop that
arrives before its owner, or an owner who arrives before their laptop, makes for a
poor first impression.

## Frequently asked questions

**What should I wear?** Whatever you are comfortable in. Some people dress up for
merchant meetings; nobody does otherwise.

**What are the working hours?** Most teams work roughly 09:30 to 17:30 UK time, with
flexibility agreed with your manager. Teams on rotas, such as Merchant Support and the
on-call engineers, have their own patterns.

**Can I work from home in my first week?** Yes if you are a remote joiner; otherwise
we suggest spending at least your first day and your team's office days in person.

**My laptop has not arrived.** If you are remote and the courier has not delivered by
the afternoon before your first day, write to `people@quillmoor.example`. We will
move your welcome call, never your start date or your pay.

**Who do I ask about something not on this page?** Your buddy first, then your
manager, then the People team at `people@quillmoor.example`.
