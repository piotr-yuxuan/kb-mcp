title: "Performance reviews"
---

We hold two review cycles a year, in **April and October**. Both are conversations
between you and your manager, prepared with feedback from the people you work with.
They are different in purpose: April looks back over the whole year and decides pay
and promotion; October is a mid-year check on progress and development.

*Owner: Rhiannon Dace, Head of people. Last reviewed: 21 September 2026, before the
October 2026 cycle.*

## The two cycles

| | April: annual review | October: mid-year review |
|---|---|---|
| Looks at | The whole fiscal year, April to March | The six months since April |
| Feedback from peers | Yes, three to five peers | Optional |
| Rating | Yes | No |
| Pay and promotion | Decided in this cycle | Promotions only in exceptional cases; no pay review |
| Recorded in | Haven | Haven |

The April cycle follows the fiscal year, which starts on 1 April, so that pay
decisions line up with the budget for the new year.

## Timeline

Each cycle takes about four weeks. Dates for the current cycle are announced in the
all-hands meeting and by email at least a fortnight before it opens.

1. **Week 1: self-review.** You write a short review in Haven: what you achieved,
   what you learnt, what got in the way, what you want to work on next. Half a page
   is enough.
2. **Week 1: peer feedback.** For the April cycle, you and your manager choose three
   to five people to give feedback. Each answers three questions: what should this
   person keep doing, start doing, and do differently?
3. **Week 2: manager assessment.** Your manager writes their assessment, using the
   expectations for your level. Engineers' expectations are in the
   [Engineering career ladder](../engineering/career-ladder.md); other functions have
   theirs on their own pages.
4. **Week 3: calibration.** Managers in each department meet to compare assessments,
   so that the same standard applies across teams. The People team facilitates.
5. **Week 4: conversation.** You and your manager meet for an hour to discuss the
   review. You see the written assessment at least a day before, and you can add your
   own comments to it in Haven.

## Ratings (April only)

| Rating | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Exceptional | Consistently well beyond the expectations of the level |
| Strong | Meets the expectations of the level fully, and goes beyond in places |
| Developing | Meets most expectations, with clear areas to grow |
| Not yet meeting | Falls short in important areas; comes with a written support plan |

Most people are rated Strong. A rating is about the year's work, not about you.

## Pay and promotion

Pay changes and promotions decided in the April cycle take effect from 1 June and
appear in that month's pay; the dates are on [Payroll dates](payroll-dates.md). Your
manager tells you the outcome in person before you receive the letter in Haven.

A promotion needs a written case from your manager, showing that you have worked at
the next level for some time, reviewed in calibration. Promotions in the October cycle
are possible only where a role has changed substantially, with the department head's
approval.

## Special cases

- **New joiners.** If you are still in your [probation period](probation.md) when a
  cycle opens, your probation reviews take its place. If you joined after 1 January,
  your April review is shorter, and you are included in the pay review on a pro rata
  basis.
- **Parental and long-term leave.** If you are on leave during a cycle, you are
  reviewed on the time you worked and included in the pay review. See the
  [Parental leave policy](parental-leave.md).
- **Changing manager.** If you changed manager in the last three months, your former
  manager contributes to the assessment.
- **Disagreement.** If you disagree with your review, add your comments in Haven and
  talk to your manager's manager. If that does not settle it, the People team will
  arrange a review by a manager from another department.
