This policy sets out how much annual leave you have, how to book it and what happens
to days you do not use. It applies to everyone employed by Quillmoor Ltd on a UK
contract, whether you work at Quill House or remotely. It has been effective from
1 January 2024 and replaced the [2022 policy](annual-leave-uk-2022.md), which is kept
only for reference.

If you are employed in France, your leave follows French law and our French
agreement instead: see [Congés payés et RTT (France)](conges-payes-rtt.md).

| Topic | Rule |

| Entitlement | 27 days a year plus bank holidays |
| Leave year | The calendar year, 1 January to 31 December |
| Carry-over | Up to 8 days, to be used by 31 March of the next year |

## Who this covers

This policy covers permanent and fixed-term employees on a UK contract, including
part-time colleagues and anyone in their probation period. You can book annual leave
from your first week; there is no waiting period. Contractors and agency workers are
not covered: their terms are in their own agreements, and their Quillmoor contact
manager can explain them.

Interns on a UK contract are covered, pro rata for the length of their placement.

## Your entitlement

A full-time employee has **27 days a year plus bank holidays**. A full-time week at
Quillmoor is five days, Monday to Friday, 37.5 hours.

Bank holidays are the public bank holidays of the nation named in your contract. Most
of us are contracted in England and follow the bank holidays of England and Wales;
colleagues contracted in Scotland or Northern Ireland follow the bank holidays there,
which differ in number and date. Haven loads the right calendar for you, so you never
need to book bank holidays yourself.

If your team rota requires you to work on one of the bank holidays as a normal
scheduled day, you receive a replacement day, which Haven adds to your balance once your manager
confirms the rota. Being on call over bank holidays is different: call-outs and
allowances are covered by the [on-call rota and paging policy](../incidents/on-call.md).

Leave builds up through the year at 2.25 days a month, but you do not have to wait for
it: you can book your whole year's entitlement from 1 January. The only time the
accrual matters is when you leave part-way through a year (see "Leaving part-way
through the year" below).

### Part-time colleagues

If you work fewer than five days a week, you receive a pro rata share of both the 27
days and the bank holidays. Haven holds your balance in hours, because part-time
patterns rarely fit whole days, and rounds up to the nearest half day.

Worked example: Dev works Monday, Tuesday and Wednesday, 22.5 hours a week.

| Element | Calculation | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Annual leave | 27 × 3 / 5 = 16.2 days | 16.5 days, rounded up |
| Bank holidays | 8 × 3 / 5 = 4.8 days | 5 days, rounded up |
| Total | | 21.5 days, held as 161.25 hours |

When one of the bank holidays falls on one of Dev's working days, Haven deducts it
from that total automatically. In a year when many bank holidays fall on Mondays, Dev uses more
of the allowance on bank holidays and has fewer days to book freely; in a year when
few do, Dev has more. This is how the pro rata share keeps part-time colleagues level
with full-time ones, whichever days they work.

If your working pattern changes during the year, the People team recalculates your
balance from the date of the change. Raise the change in Haven and the recalculation
appears within a week.

### Joining part-way through the year

If you join after 1 January, your entitlement for the first year is pro rata for the
complete and partial months you work, rounded up to the nearest half day.

Worked example: Priti joins on 1 June 2026. She works seven months of the year, so
her entitlement is 27 × 7 / 12 = 15.75 days, rounded up to 16 days, plus the
bank holidays that fall between June and December. Her balance appears in Haven on her
first day.

## The leave year and carry-over

The leave year is the calendar year. Balances reset on 1 January.

You may **carry over up to 8 days** of unused annual leave into the next year. Haven
does this automatically overnight on 31 December: you do not need to ask. Carried
days **must be used by 31 March**. Any carried days still unused at the end of 31
March are lost, and any unused days above the 8 are lost on 31 December.

When you book leave between 1 January and 31 March, Haven takes the carried days
first, so that you do not lose them by accident.

Worked example: on 31 December 2025, Tom had 11 days unused.

1. Haven carried 8 days into 2026 and removed the other 3.
2. Tom booked 4 days in February 2026; Haven took all 4 from the carried days.
3. On 31 March 2026, the remaining 4 carried days lapsed. His 27 days for 2026 were
   untouched.

We do not pay for untaken annual leave, except in your final pay when you leave.

### Exceptions to the carry-over limit

The limit gives way in two cases, where the law protects your leave:

- **Sickness.** If long-term sickness prevented you from taking your leave, you may
  carry over the statutory part of your untaken leave beyond 31 March. See
  [Sickness absence](sickness-absence.md).
- **Parental and other family leave.** If you could not take your leave because you
  were on maternity, adoption, shared parental or other family leave, you may carry
  over all of it, to be used after you return. See the
  [parental leave policy](parental-leave.md).

In both cases the People team sets the extra carry-over and its expiry date in Haven;
talk to them before 31 December if you think it applies to you.

Apart from these, managers cannot approve extra carry-over. If a project kept you from
taking leave, raise it with your manager in good time, ideally by October, so that you
can plan your remaining days.

## Booking leave

Book all annual leave in Haven, from the web or the mobile app. The step-by-step guide
is in [Using Haven](haven-how-to.md). Your manager receives the request and approves
or declines it; you receive an email either way.

### Notice

- For **more than 5 consecutive days** of annual leave, give at least **two weeks'
  notice**: the start of your leave must be at least two weeks after the date of your
  request.
- For shorter absences, give as much notice as you reasonably can. A single day
  booked the day before is fine if your team can cover it.
- Bank holidays and weekends inside your leave do not count towards the 5 consecutive
  days: a Monday to Friday week that includes one of the bank holidays is 4 days of
  annual leave and needs no special notice.

Worked example: Ana wants Monday 3 to Friday 14 August 2026, which is 10 days. She
must request it by Monday 20 July 2026 at the latest. A request made on 24 July would
be short notice: her manager may still approve it, but is not expected to.

### How managers decide

Managers approve or decline within three working days. They approve unless the
absence would leave the team unable to meet a commitment, such as cover for a
customer-facing rota, the month-end close in Finance, or a launch date agreed in
advance. When two requests clash, the earlier request normally wins.

A manager who declines must say why in Haven and suggest other dates. If you think a
decision is unfair, raise it with your manager's manager, then with the People team.

### Before you go

- Set an out-of-office reply on your email and a status in chat.
- Hand over anything that cannot wait, with names, in your team's channel.
- If you are on an on-call rota, arrange a swap before booking; the rules for swaps
  are in the [on-call policy](../incidents/on-call.md).
- Leave your laptop locked and charged at home or locked away at the office.

### Cancelling or changing leave

You can cancel leave that has not started in Haven; the days go back to your balance.
To change dates, cancel and book again, and the notice rule applies to the new
booking.

Quillmoor can ask you to cancel or move approved leave only in exceptional
circumstances, such as a serious incident that needs your particular skills, and only
with your agreement. If you agree and lose money as a result, for example on a
non-refundable booking, the company reimburses it through the
[expenses policy](../finance/expenses.md) process with your manager's approval.

## Leaving part-way through the year

When you leave, your entitlement is pro rata to your last working day, plus any
carried days still valid. The difference between that and what you have taken is
settled in your final pay.

Worked example: Sam's last day is 30 September 2026. Sam's entitlement for 2026 is
27 × 9 / 12 = 20.25 days, rounded up to 20.5 days.

- If Sam has taken 15 days, the 5.5 days outstanding are paid in the final payslip.
- If Sam has taken 23 days, the 2.5 days taken beyond entitlement are deducted from
  the final payslip.

Your manager may ask you to take outstanding leave during your notice period instead.
The rest of the leaving process is on [Leaving Quillmoor](leaving.md).

## When annual leave meets other absences

| Situation | What happens |
|---|---|
| You fall ill during annual leave | Tell your manager as you would for any sickness and record it in Haven. The days you were ill are returned to your balance. |
| You are on parental leave | Annual leave keeps building up at the normal rate. You can take it before or after your parental leave. |
| You work from abroad | Working days abroad are working days, not annual leave. See [Working from abroad](working-abroad.md). |
| You travel for work over a weekend | No annual leave is used; agree a day off afterwards with your manager. |
| Bereavement, jury service, emergencies with dependants | Covered by special leave and never deducted from your annual leave. Ask the People team. |

## Pay during annual leave

You are paid your normal salary during annual leave. If you receive regular
allowances, for example the on-call allowance, the payroll team includes your average
over the previous 52 weeks in the pay for your leave days, as the law requires.
Payroll dates are on [Payroll dates](payroll-dates.md).

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I take annual leave during my probation period?**
Yes. Your entitlement starts on your first day and the usual notice rule applies.

**Can I buy or sell days?**
No. We reviewed it when this policy was written and chose a higher entitlement for
everyone instead.

**Can I borrow from next year?**
No. You can only book against the current year's balance and any valid carried days.
You can, however, book next year's dates now: Haven accepts requests up to 18 months
ahead and charges them to the right year.

**I booked days in December that I now cannot take. Do I lose them?**
Up to 8 unused days carry over automatically. Beyond that, speak to your manager
before 31 December; managers cannot extend the carry-over, but they can help you
rearrange work so that you take the days.

**My team is going to a conference over a weekend that includes bank holidays. Does
it use my leave?**
No. Business travel is working time. See the "When annual leave meets other
absences" table above.

**Where do I see how many days I have left?**
On the Haven home page, under "My leave". It shows the year's entitlement, carried
days with their expiry date, days booked and days remaining.

**Who checks the balances at the start of the year?**
The payroll team checks every carry-over in the first week of January and contacts
anyone whose balance looks wrong. If you spot a mistake yourself, write to
`people@quillmoor.example`.
