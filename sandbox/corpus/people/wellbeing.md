title: "Wellbeing support"
---

Work is a large part of life, and life does not always go to plan. Here is the
support Quillmoor offers when you need someone to talk to, some practical help, or
simply a quiet place for twenty minutes. All of it is confidential, and none of it
needs your manager's approval.

*Owner: Rhiannon Dace, Head of people. Last reviewed: 7 September 2026.*

## Someone to talk to, any time

Our **employee assistance programme** is a free, confidential service run by an
independent provider. It is open to every employee, and to the adults in your
household, at any hour of any day.

- **Phone counselling**, straight away, in English or French.
- **Up to six counselling sessions** per issue, by video, phone or in person.
- **Practical advice** on money worries, debt, housing, legal questions, caring for
  relatives and relationship difficulties.

The phone number and access code are on the Haven home page, under "Wellbeing", and
on a card in the wellbeing rooms. Quillmoor receives only anonymous usage figures
from the provider: we never know who called or why.

## People in the office

**Mental health first aiders** are colleagues trained to listen, to recognise when
someone is struggling and to point them towards help. They are not therapists. You
can talk to any of them, in any team; their names are listed with the physical first
aiders on [First aiders and the wellbeing room](../workplace/first-aiders.md).

The **wellbeing rooms** in London and Paris are quiet rooms you can book, or use
when free, to rest, pray, express milk or take a difficult call. The same page
explains where they are.

## Health support

- Private medical insurance includes mental health treatment on referral, for UK
  employees; see [Benefits in the UK](benefits-uk.md). French colleagues are covered
  through the [mutuelle](mutuelle-et-prevoyance.md).
- Free flu vaccinations are offered in both offices every October, and as vouchers for
  remote colleagues.
- Free eye tests are part of the UK benefits.

## When you are not well

If you are unwell, physically or mentally, the rules are the same: tell your manager
and take the time you need. See [Sickness absence](sickness-absence.md). If you would
rather not tell your manager why, you do not have to.

## Talking to your manager

Managers are not expected to be counsellors, but they are expected to listen and to
make reasonable changes to help: a temporary change of hours, a lighter workload, a
quieter desk, more working from home. Most of these can be agreed in a one-to-one. If
you would rather start with someone else, the People team is always available at
`people@quillmoor.example`, in confidence.

## Something at work is the problem

If the problem is how you are being treated at work, you can raise it with your
manager, your manager's manager or the People team. If you have concerns about
wrongdoing, see [Whistleblowing](../legal/whistleblowing.md).

## In an emergency

If you or someone else is in immediate danger, call the emergency services first: 999
in the UK, 112 in France and across Europe.
