Becoming a parent changes a great deal, and the last thing you should worry about is
the paperwork. This policy explains the leave and pay Quillmoor offers when you
become a parent, by birth, adoption or surrogacy, and how to arrange it. If you only
read one part, read "Summary" and then talk to the People team: we would rather hear
from you early, even before you have told your team.

You can contact the People team in confidence at `people@quillmoor.example`. Nothing
you tell us is shared with your manager until you are ready.

## Summary

| You are | Leave | Pay from Quillmoor |
|---|---|---|
| The primary carer | Up to 52 weeks | **20 weeks at full pay**, then statutory pay to week 39, then unpaid |
| The other parent | 6 weeks, in up to three blocks | **6 weeks at full pay** |
| Either parent, sharing | Shared parental leave, converted from the primary carer's leave | Full pay up to the per-person cap, then statutory shared parental pay |

There is no minimum length of service for Quillmoor's enhanced pay: it applies from
your first day. Statutory pay has its own qualifying conditions, which the payroll team
checks for you.

## Who is who

**The primary carer** is the parent who gives birth; or, in an adoption, the adopter
who takes the main caring role; or, in a surrogacy arrangement, the intended parent
who will be the main carer and who has applied, or will apply, for a parental order.

**The other parent** is the primary carer's spouse, civil partner or partner, or the
child's other parent, who will share responsibility for bringing up the child. In an
adoption, it is the second adopter.

These terms are about caring roles, not about gender. Same-sex couples, adopters and
intended parents through surrogacy have exactly the same entitlements as anyone else.

## Leave for the primary carer

### How much leave and pay

You can take up to 52 weeks of leave.

- **Weeks 1 to 20: full pay.** Quillmoor pays your normal salary. Any statutory pay
  you are entitled to is included in it, not added on top.
- **Weeks 21 to 39: statutory pay**, at the rate set each April, if you qualify for
  it.
- **Weeks 40 to 52: unpaid leave.** Your job and your benefits are protected
  throughout.

You decide how much of the 52 weeks to take. Most people at Quillmoor take between
nine and twelve months.

We do not ask you to repay any of the enhanced pay if you decide not to come back
after your leave.

### When it starts

For a birth, you can start your leave at any time from the 11th week before the
expected week of childbirth. Leave starts automatically if your baby arrives early,
or if you are off sick with a pregnancy-related illness in the four weeks before the
expected week. The two weeks immediately after the birth are compulsory leave by law.

For an adoption, leave can start on the day the child is placed with you or up to two
weeks before. For a surrogacy, it starts on the day of the birth or the day after.

### Telling us

| What | When |
|---|---|
| Tell your manager or the People team that you are expecting a child | By the end of the 15th week before the expected week of childbirth, or as soon as you reasonably can |
| Give us the medical certificate of the expected date | At the same time, or as soon as your midwife or doctor issues it |
| Confirm your start date | At least 28 days before you want your leave to start |
| Adoption: tell us you have been matched | Within 7 days of being notified of the match |
| Change your return date | At least 8 weeks before the new date |

Record your leave in Haven once you have agreed the dates with the People team; the
step-by-step guide is in [Using Haven](haven-how-to.md).

### Worked example

Amira's baby is due on Monday 17 March 2025. She starts her leave two weeks earlier,
on Monday 3 March 2025, and takes the full year.

| Period | Dates | Pay |
|---|---|---|
| Weeks 1 to 20 | 3 March to 20 July 2025 | Full pay |
| Weeks 21 to 39 | 21 July to 30 November 2025 | Statutory pay |
| Weeks 40 to 52 | 1 December 2025 to 1 March 2026 | Unpaid |
| Return | Monday 2 March 2026 | Normal pay |

Amira keeps building up annual leave throughout the 52 weeks, and she can attach some
of it to the end of her leave before she returns.

## Leave for the other parent

You can take **6 weeks at full pay**. You can take them:

- as one block of 6 weeks, or in up to three blocks of at least one week each;
- at any time within 12 months of the birth or placement.

Give your manager at least four weeks' notice of each block, or as much notice as
you can if the baby arrives early. The 6 weeks include any statutory paternity leave
you are entitled to; you do not take that separately.

### Worked example

Jonah's partner gives birth on Monday 14 April 2025. Jonah takes:

1. two weeks straight away, from 14 to 27 April 2025;
2. four weeks in the summer, from 4 to 31 August 2025, when his partner's family
   visit ends.

All six weeks are at full pay. Had Jonah kept a block for later, it would have had to
end by 13 April 2026, 12 months after the birth.

## Shared parental leave

Shared parental leave lets parents share the year of leave between them, in blocks,
instead of the primary carer taking it all. It can suit families where both parents
want to spend a long stretch at home, or where the primary carer wants to go back to
work earlier.

### How it works

1. The primary carer ends their own leave early, by giving notice of the date it will
   end. The two compulsory weeks after a birth must still be taken.
2. The weeks of leave left over become shared parental leave, which either parent can
   take: at the same time, or one after the other, in up to three blocks each.
3. Each block needs **8 weeks' notice**, and both parents must confirm their plans in
   writing. The People team has a form in Haven that does this for you.

The legal rules are detailed; the People team will check your eligibility and plans
before you commit to anything.

### Pay during shared parental leave

Quillmoor pays shared parental leave taken within 12 months of the birth or placement
at full pay, up to a cap: **no employee receives more than 20 weeks at full pay for
the same child**, counting every type of parental leave together. Weeks beyond the
cap are paid at the statutory shared parental rate while it lasts, then unpaid.

This means that the other parent's 6 weeks count towards their own cap, and that a
primary carer who ends their leave after 12 weeks still has 8 weeks of full pay
available if they later take shared parental leave.

### Worked example: both parents at Quillmoor

Ruth gives birth on Monday 10 March 2025. Her partner Ben also works at Quillmoor.

| Who | Leave | Dates | Pay |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ruth | Primary carer leave, then ends it | 10 March to 1 June 2025 (12 weeks) | Full pay |
| Ben | Other-parent leave | 10 March to 20 April 2025 (6 weeks) | Full pay |
| Ben | Shared parental leave | 2 June to 7 September 2025 (14 weeks) | Full pay, reaching his 20-week cap |
| Ruth | Back at work | From 2 June 2025 | Normal pay |

If Ben had stayed at home until the end of September, his extra three weeks would
have been paid at the statutory shared parental rate. If the family's other parent
works for another employer, that employer's policy decides their pay; Quillmoor's
cap applies only to Quillmoor employees.

## Keeping in touch

During your leave you can work up to 10 keeping-in-touch days, plus up to 20 more
during shared parental leave, without ending your leave. They are entirely optional
for you; your manager may suggest them, for example for a team away day, but cannot
require them. Each day is paid at your normal daily rate, on top of any statutory
pay, and you record it in Haven.

Your manager will agree with you before you go how often, and how, you would like to
hear from the team. Some people want the monthly newsletter and nothing else; others
like to be invited to the summer party. Both are fine.

## Coming back

- **Your role.** If you return within 26 weeks, you come back to the same role. After
  that, you come back to the same role or, if that is not reasonably possible, a
  suitable one on terms no less favourable.
- **A gentle start.** For your first four weeks back, you may work 80% of your usual
  hours on full pay. Agree the pattern with your manager.
- **Flexible working.** You can ask for a change to your hours, days or place of work
  at any time. Managers must consider every request seriously and answer within two
  months. Many of our part-time patterns started this way.
- **Feeding and expressing.** The wellbeing rooms in our offices can be booked for
  this; see [First aiders and the wellbeing room](../workplace/first-aiders.md).
- **Your laptop and access.** Your accounts stay active during your leave, so that you
  can read the newsletter if you wish. If your access was suspended, IT restores it
  on your first morning back.

## Your benefits during leave

| Benefit | During paid leave | During unpaid leave |
|---|---|---|
| Annual leave | Keeps building up | Keeps building up |
| Pension | Employer contributions on your normal salary | Paused, unless you ask to continue your own |
| Private medical insurance | Continues | Continues |
| Life assurance | Continues | Continues |
| Learning budget | Can be used, including for a course during keeping-in-touch days | Can be used |

Annual leave you could not take because of parental leave can be carried over beyond
the usual limit; see the [Annual leave policy (UK)](annual-leave-uk.md). The full list
of benefits is on [Benefits in the UK](benefits-uk.md).

## Other situations

**Antenatal and adoption appointments.** Both parents can take paid time off for
antenatal appointments, including classes recommended by a midwife, and for adoption
meetings. Tell your manager; no need to record them as leave.

**Pregnancy loss.** If you or your partner experience a miscarriage before the 24th
week of pregnancy, both of you can take two weeks of paid compassionate leave,
straight away or later. A loss after the 24th week, or a stillbirth, gives you the
full entitlements in this policy as if your baby had lived.

**Neonatal care.** If your baby needs neonatal care for more than a week in the first
28 days, each parent can take up to 12 weeks of neonatal care leave at full pay, on
top of the leave above.

**Fertility treatment.** Both partners can take paid time for appointments. Talk to
your manager, or to the People team if you would rather not.

**Twins and more.** Entitlements are per pregnancy or placement, not per child.

## Colleagues on French contracts

Colleagues employed by Quillmoor SAS receive the French statutory leave (congé de
maternité, de paternité et d'accueil de l'enfant, d'adoption), with Quillmoor
maintaining full salary and receiving the social security daily allowances in your
place. Quillmoor then adds paid leave, so that the full-pay totals match this policy:
20 weeks for the primary carer and 6 weeks for the other parent. Congé parental
d'éducation, which is unpaid, is available under French law. Noémie Ferrand, HR
adviser for France, handles every request; leave is recorded in Haven. For how parental leave
affects congés payés and RTT, see [Congés payés et RTT (France)](conges-payes-rtt.md).

## Frequently asked questions

**I am in my probation period. Am I covered?**
Yes. There is no minimum service for Quillmoor's enhanced pay, and your probation
pauses during your leave.

**Can I take parental leave and then annual leave straight after?**
Yes, and many people do. Book the annual leave in Haven as usual.

**My partner and I both work at Quillmoor. Can we both be off at the same time?**
Yes. The other parent's 6 weeks and shared parental leave can overlap with the
primary carer's leave.

**Will my bonus or pay rise be affected?**
No. If a pay review happens during your leave, you are included in it, and your
performance is assessed on the time you worked. See
[Performance reviews](performance-reviews.md).

**Who do I talk to first?**
Anyone you are comfortable with: your manager, or the People team at
`people@quillmoor.example`.
