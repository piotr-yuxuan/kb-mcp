You can work from another country for **up to 20 working days a year**, for example
to extend a family visit or to spend a few weeks near relatives. This page explains
how to ask, where you can go and what to check before you do.

## What counts

The 20 working days are counted per calendar year, from 1 January to 31 December.

- Any day on which you work from outside your country of employment counts as one
  day, even if you work only part of it.
- Weekends, bank holidays and days of annual leave spent abroad do not count, because
  you are not working.
- **Business travel does not count.** If Quillmoor sends you to a merchant or a
  conference, that is business travel, booked as described in
  [Booking business travel](../workplace/business-travel.md).
- The days do not need to be consecutive, and unused days do not carry over.

Working from abroad is not annual leave: you work your normal hours and stay
reachable. Attaching some working days to a period of annual leave is fine and is the
most common use.

## How to ask

1. Check the destination against the restricted country list on the Security pages.
   If it is on the list, the answer is no.
2. Request "Work from abroad" in Haven, with the dates and the country, at least two
   weeks before you travel. Haven shows how many days you have left for the year.
3. Your manager approves it, checking that your work can be done from there, in that
   time zone.
4. If your role gives you access to the card data environment or to production
   administration, the request also goes to Callum Ibe, Head of security, who may
   limit what you can do from abroad.

## Before you go

- **Your devices.** Take only your company laptop and phone, and follow
  [Travelling with company devices](../security/travel-with-devices.md). Use the
  company VPN on any network you do not control.
- **Your hours.** Agree with your team which hours you will overlap. Most teams ask
  for at least four hours of overlap with UK time.
- **On-call.** You may stay on an on-call rota from abroad only if your manager agrees
  that your connection is reliable. Otherwise swap your shifts before you go.
- **Right to work.** You are responsible for making sure you may enter the country
  and work there remotely for the time you plan. If you are unsure, ask the People
  team before booking anything.
- **Insurance.** Our business travel insurance covers working-abroad trips within
  these rules; the policy details are on the Finance pages.

## If something happens while you are away

If you fall ill, tell your manager by 10:00 your local time, as you would at home; the
rest is in [Sickness absence](sickness-absence.md). If you cannot return as planned,
because of a cancelled flight for example, tell your manager and the People team the
same day. Days you are stuck abroad count towards your 20 only if you work them.

## Worked example

Hana, on a UK contract, plans three weeks with her family in Lisbon in August
2026: one week of annual leave, then two weeks of working from there, ten working
days.

- She requests the ten days in Haven on 6 July, more than two weeks ahead.
- Her manager approves; she has no production access, so no further approval is
  needed.
- Her week of annual leave is booked separately, under the
  [Annual leave policy (UK)](annual-leave-uk.md).
- After the trip she has ten working days left for the rest of 2026.

## Longer stays

More than 20 working days in a year, or any stay that would make you work from abroad
for more than four weeks in a row, can create tax and legal obligations for you and
for Quillmoor. We consider these only in exceptional circumstances, with the approval
of the Head of people and the General counsel. Ask at least two months ahead.

Moving to another country permanently is a different conversation: talk to your
manager and the People team.
