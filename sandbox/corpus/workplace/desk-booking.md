| Last reviewed | 2026-09-28 |

Apart from a handful of assigned desks, none of our desks belongs to anyone in particular. You book a desk for the day you are coming in, in Perch, and give it back when you leave. This page explains how hybrid working fits together, how desk booking works, and what happens in the awkward cases.

## The short version

1. **Book every desk in Perch.** No booking, no desk, even if the one you like is empty.
2. **Team days are Tuesday and Thursday**, in both offices.
3. **You can book up to 14 days ahead.**
4. **Check in by 10:00.** A desk not checked into by 10:00 is released, and anyone can then book it.

Everything below is detail and edge cases.

## How hybrid working works here

Our offices are for working together. Focused work can happen anywhere; the things that go better in a room, such as planning, design critiques, onboarding, pairing on a hard problem and simply getting to know each other, are what we come in for.

### Team days

Tuesday and Thursday are team days for the whole company, in London and in Paris. Teams put their planning sessions, workshops, reviews and socials on those days, and new joiners are paired with their buddies on them.

If you live within reach of an office, we expect you to be there on both team days, unless you are on leave, off sick, travelling, or have agreed something different with your manager. Nobody checks attendance badge by badge; we ask managers to talk to their teams about what works.

### The other three days

Monday, Wednesday and Friday are yours to arrange with your team. Some teams add a third office day of their own, and Merchant Support follows its rota (see [Support shift patterns](../support/shift-patterns.md)). Others are rarely in on those days. Either is fine, as long as the people who rely on you know where to find you.

### Remote colleagues

About 60 of us work remotely, in the UK and in France. You are welcome in either office on any day, and you book a desk exactly as everyone else does. If your visit needs travel, see [Booking business travel](business-travel.md).

### Formal changes

Desk booking is not the place to change your contract. A formal change to your working pattern, such as compressed hours or a different number of days, goes through your manager and the People team at `people@quillmoor.example`. Working from another country for a while is covered by [Working from abroad](../people/working-abroad.md).

## Perch

Perch is our booking tool for desks, meeting rooms and visitors. You will find it at `perch.quillmoor.example` and as an app on company phones; you sign in with your usual company account.

Perch shows a floor plan for floors 3, 4 and 5 of Quill House and for the 4th floor of Maison Lanterne. Each desk is coloured by its state: free, booked, checked in, or assigned. By default, other people can see your name on the desk you have booked, which makes it easy to sit near the colleagues you need. You can hide your name from other staff in your Perch settings; reception and the fire wardens still see it, because they use the day's list in an evacuation (see [Fire safety and evacuation](fire-safety.md)).

Meeting rooms are booked in Perch too, under rules of their own: see [Meeting rooms](meeting-rooms.md). So are visitors: see [Visitors and deliveries](visitors-and-deliveries.md).

## Booking a desk

1. Open Perch and choose the office and the date.
2. Filter by neighbourhood to find your team, or by desk type if you need something specific.
3. Pick a desk and confirm. Perch sends a calendar invitation with the desk number.

### The booking window

You can book up to 14 days ahead. On a Tuesday, for example, you can book any day up to and including the Tuesday two weeks later. A new day opens for booking at midnight, UK time for London and Paris time for Paris.

The window is the same for everyone, including team leads, assistants and the leadership team. For something that needs space further ahead, such as a company event or a visit from a large group, talk to the Workplace team about the Commons in London or l'Atelier in Paris instead.

### What you can book

- Whole days only. A booking runs from opening time to closing time.
- One desk per person per day.
- Several days in one go: select each day in the calendar, as long as each one falls within the 14 days.
- The same desk week after week, whenever it is free. There is no standing booking that renews itself.

### Desk types

| Type | Where | What you get |
|---|---|---|
| Standard | Every neighbourhood | One 27-inch monitor, a single-cable dock, an adjustable chair |
| Dual screen | Floors 4 and 5 in London, the east wing in Paris | Two monitors and a dock |
| Sit-stand | At least four in every neighbourhood | An electric height-adjustable desk with one monitor |
| Focus | The quiet zone on floor 4 in London, the far end of the east wing in Paris | A standard desk in an area with no calls and no meetings |
| Assigned | Marked grey in Perch | Not bookable; see "Assigned desks" below |

## Checking in

Check in once you are at your desk, by 10:00 on the day:

- scan the code on the desk's label with the Perch app, or
- open the booking in Perch on your laptop, while connected to `QM-Staff`, and choose "Check in".

Perch reminds you at 09:30 if you have not checked in.

At 10:00, every booked desk that nobody has checked into is released. The booking is cancelled, your calendar invitation disappears, and the desk shows as free for anyone to book. In Paris, the deadline is 10:00 Paris time.

We brought in checking in because, before it, around one desk in eight booked for a team day stood empty all day while colleagues looked for somewhere to sit. Releasing at 10:00 gives the morning's latecomers a fair chance at a desk without taking it away from anyone who is merely running a few minutes late.

> [!TIP]
> Check in when you sit down rather than when you remember. Most releases happen to people who were at their desk by nine and simply forgot.

## When plans change

**You are not coming in.** Cancel the booking in Perch as soon as you know. On team days there is usually someone on the waiting list.

**The neighbourhood is full.** Join the waiting list for it in Perch. When a desk there is cancelled or released, Perch tells the first person on the list, who then has to book it like anyone else. The waiting list does not book on your behalf.

**You will arrive after 10:00.** Your booking will be released; the deadline cannot be extended. When you arrive, book any free desk in Perch and check in straight away. Released desks are free to anyone, so there is often one in your own neighbourhood. If you know in advance that you will be late, for an appointment for instance, it is kinder to leave the desk unbooked and book on arrival.

**You are ill.** Cancel the booking if you can; if you cannot, it is released at 10:00 anyway. Tell your manager as described in [Sickness absence](../people/sickness-absence.md).

**Your booked desk is occupied.** Show the person your booking in Perch. If they have checked in to it by mistake, they will usually move; if not, ask reception to help.

**The desk is broken.** Book another, check in there, and report the fault to `workplace@quillmoor.example` with the desk number.

**Bookings keep being released.** Perch counts every booking released at 10:00. If it happens to you often, someone from the Workplace team will get in touch, not to tell you off, but to see whether the booking pattern is working for you.

## Team neighbourhoods

Each team has a home neighbourhood: a group of desks near the rest of the team, signed at the end of the row and outlined on the Perch floor plan.

| Office | Floor or wing | Teams |
|---|---|---|
| London | Floor 3 | People, Finance, Legal, Merchant Support, Workplace |
| London | Floor 4 | Payments Core, Payouts, Ledger, Platform, Site Reliability, Security |
| London | Floor 5 | Data Platform, Risk ML, Design System, Merchant Experience, Product |
| Paris | East wing | Payments Core, Risk ML, Data Platform, Product |
| Paris | West wing | Merchant Support (France), Finance, People |

On team days, a neighbourhood's desks can only be booked by members of that team until 17:00 the day before. After that, they are open to anyone. On Mondays, Wednesdays and Fridays anyone can book anywhere.

When a team has more people in than its neighbourhood holds, the overflow tables in the Commons in London, or in l'Atelier in Paris, are open to everyone and are not booked.

### Block bookings

Team leads can book several desks at once for named members of their team, for a planning day for example. The same 14-day window applies, each desk must have a name against it, and each person still checks in by 10:00. Holding a row of desks without names is not allowed: those desks would be released at 10:00 regardless.

## Assigned desks

A small number of desks in each office are assigned to one person. They are for colleagues with an agreed workplace adjustment: a specialist chair, a particular monitor set-up, a desk near an exit or a lift, or a quieter spot. Assigned desks are shown in grey in Perch, cannot be booked by anyone else, and are never released at 10:00. When the holder is away, the desk stays empty.

To ask for one, talk to your manager and the People team; Gwen Talbot or Élodie Vasseur will then set it up. You do not need to share medical details with the Workplace team.

## Booking for someone else

- **A new joiner.** Their manager can book desks for their first days, before they have Perch on their own laptop. The rest of their first fortnight is described in [New joiner onboarding](../people/onboarding.md).
- **A colleague from the other office, or a remote colleague.** Book in their name. They check in themselves, so make sure they have Perch on their phone or laptop.
- **A visitor.** Visitors do not get desks. Register them in Perch as visitors and book a meeting room. A contractor who will work alongside us for weeks gets a Perch account through their sponsor and then books like everyone else.

## At your desk

- Plug in to the dock with the single cable; the monitor, keyboard and network come with it.
- Adjust the chair and the desk height to suit you, and leave the monitors where they are. Please do not move monitors, chairs or docks between desks.
- Leave the desk clear when you go. The cleaners wipe down clear desks every evening and skip desks with things on them. Anything you leave overnight belongs in a locker; see the [London office guide](london-office.md) or the [Paris office guide](bureau-de-paris.md).
- Take calls in a phone booth or a meeting room, not at your desk.
- The rules about your laptop itself, such as when it should lock, are in the [Laptop security baseline](../security/laptop-baseline.md).

## In Paris

Everything on this page applies to Maison Lanterne. Paris has 48 bookable desks for around 60 colleagues, so on team days it fills up quickly: book early and cancel promptly. Checking in closes at 10:00 Paris time. Élodie Vasseur looks after desk booking in Paris and can explain any of it in French.

## Floor 5 in London

While floor 5 is being refurbished, the desks in the area under works are hidden in Perch and cannot be booked. The [floor 5 refurbishment notice](floor-5-refurbishment.md) says which teams are sitting where in the meantime.

## When Perch is down

If Perch is unavailable, sit in your team's neighbourhood or wherever is free. Nothing is released while Perch is down, and nobody will be asked to move because they could not book. Workplace posts in `#workplace-london` and `#bureau-paris` when it is back, and you then book and check in for the rest of the day.

## Bank holidays and closures

Perch does not offer days when an office is closed. That means UK bank holidays for London, French public holidays for Paris, weekends, and the closure between Christmas and New Year. A London bank holiday is a normal working day in Paris, and the reverse is also true, so check the right office before you travel.
