# Visitors and deliveries

> Owned by the Workplace team: Gwen Talbot in London, Élodie Vasseur in Paris. Last reviewed on 2026-10-01. Send corrections to `workplace@quillmoor.example`.

This page covers two things that both arrive at reception: people who do not work here, and parcels. Read the first half before you invite anyone in, and the second half before you order anything to the office. The rules apply to both offices; where Paris does things differently, the last section says so.

## Visitors

A visitor is anyone without a Quillmoor badge: a merchant, a candidate, an auditor, a supplier, a contractor, a friend coming for lunch, or a colleague's partner picking them up. If they will go past reception, they are a visitor and they need to be registered.

### Before the visit: register them in Perch

Register every visitor in Perch at least 24 hours before they arrive. Perch passes the list to reception and, in London, to the building's security desk in the lobby, which will not let unregistered guests through the gates.

1. In Perch, open **Visitors** and choose **Register a visitor**.
2. Enter the visitor's full name, their organisation, their email address, the date and the expected arrival time.
3. Name yourself as host. If you will be in a meeting when they arrive, add a second host who will be at their desk.
4. Add anything reception should know: step-free access, a hearing loop, a guide dog, a large item they are bringing, or that they are here for an interview.
5. Book the meeting room separately (see [Meeting rooms](meeting-rooms.md)). Visitors do not get desks.

Perch emails the visitor the evening before with the building's name, the entrance to use, the time reception expects them and the name of their host. Please do not send your own directions as well: two sets of instructions are worse than one.

For a group of more than ten, for example a merchant workshop or a university visit, register each person by name and tell `workplace@quillmoor.example` at least three working days ahead, so that reception can prepare badges in advance and the building can be warned.

### When 24 hours is not possible

Sometimes a visit is arranged at short notice: an urgent meeting with a merchant, a supplier coming to fix something, an interview moved at the last minute. Register them in Perch anyway, as soon as you can, and then tell reception in person or in `#workplace-london` (in Paris, `#bureau-paris`). Reception will do its best, but in London the building's security desk may ask the visitor to wait in the lobby until you come down to collect them in person. Late registrations are the exception, not a second route.

### On the day

1. The visitor gives their name at the building's security desk in the lobby, and is sent up to floor 3.
2. Quillmoor reception checks them against the Perch list, prints a visitor badge, and messages the host.
3. The host collects them from reception. Visitors do not wait unaccompanied anywhere but the reception area.

Visitors in London can use the `QM-Guest` Wi-Fi; the day's passphrase is printed on the badge. They cannot use `QM-Staff`, and nobody should connect a visitor's device to a company network or plug it into a company laptop.

### During the visit

- **Stay with your visitor.** You are responsible for them from the moment you collect them at reception to the moment they hand their badge back. If you need to step away, hand them over to a colleague, by name.
- **Keep to the meeting rooms.** Visitors meet in rooms, ideally in the four guest rooms near reception on floor 3 in London. If there is a reason to show someone round the floors, walk them yourself and keep it brief.
- **Mind the screens and whiteboards.** Clean whiteboards before a visitor comes into a room, and do not walk them past dashboards you would not show them in a meeting. What visitors may and may not see depends on how the information is classified; see [Data classification](../security/data-classification.md).
- **Confidential discussions.** If a visitor will see confidential material, make sure the right agreement is in place before the visit; [Mutual NDA: template and guidance](../legal/nda.md) explains how to get one.
- **Gifts.** If a visitor brings a gift, follow [Gifts and hospitality](../legal/gifts-and-hospitality.md).
- **Fire alarm.** If the alarm sounds, take your visitor with you to the assembly point and make sure the fire warden knows they are there. Reception's visitor list is used for the roll call.

### When they leave

Walk your visitor back to reception and make sure they hand in their badge. Perch records the time. If a visitor leaves with their badge, tell reception so that it can be switched off.

### Special cases

| Visitor | What is different |
|---|---|
| Interview candidates | The recruiter registers them in Perch and books the room; the interviewer is the host. See [Interviewing engineers](../engineering/interviewing.md) for engineering interviews. |
| Contractors doing work in the office | Register them in Perch as usual, and arrange the work with Gwen Talbot or Élodie Vasseur beforehand: anyone using tools, ladders or power in the office needs Workplace's permission. |
| Auditors and assessors on site for several days | Register each day in Perch. For a visit of more than two days, reception can issue a temporary badge that opens the guest rooms but not the working floors. |
| Former employees | They are visitors like anyone else and need a host. |
| Family and friends | Welcome at lunchtime or at the end of the day, in the Commons, with you as host. Children come in only for family days, which Workplace announces in chat. |
| Pets | Not allowed, except assistance dogs. |

### Visitor data

Perch keeps each visitor's name, organisation, email address, host and the times they arrived and left. Reception uses the list for the day's fire roll call, and nobody uses it for marketing. Records are kept only as long as [the data retention schedule](../data/retention-schedule.md) allows. Questions about visitor data go to `privacy@quillmoor.example`.

## Deliveries

### Parcels and post

Deliveries come to reception: in London, Quillmoor reception on floor 3; in Paris, the Quillmoor reception desk on the 4th floor. Couriers report to the building's security desk in the lobby, which sends them up.

When you order something to the office, address it to Quillmoor at Quill House or Maison Lanterne, with your full name, your team and your floor. A parcel addressed only to "Quillmoor" is opened by reception to find out whose it is.

Reception signs for the parcel, logs it and emails you. Collect it from reception within two working days. Parcels not collected after five working days are returned to the sender, or, if that is not possible, handed to [lost property](lost-property.md).

Reception accepts deliveries during staffed hours only, from 08:30 to 18:00 in London. Outside those hours, couriers are turned away by building security, so pick a delivery slot in working hours whenever the carrier offers one.

### Large deliveries: the loading bay

Anything too large or too heavy for reception comes in through the loading bay at the rear of Quill House instead: furniture, pallets, more than three boxes at once, equipment for a room, a delivery that needs a trolley, or anything that would not fit comfortably in a passenger lift.

1. Email `workplace@quillmoor.example` at least three working days before the delivery, with the supplier, the number and size of the items, the date and the floor they are for.
2. Workplace books a slot with the building's management. The loading bay takes deliveries from 07:30 to 10:30 and from 14:00 to 16:00, Monday to Friday, for vans and lorries up to 10 metres long.
3. Give the supplier the slot and tell them to use the service yard at the back of the building, not the main entrance.
4. Someone from Workplace, or the person who ordered the delivery, meets the driver at the loading bay and takes the goods up in the goods lift.

Unbooked large deliveries are refused by the building, and the supplier may charge for a second attempt. Goods ordered through a purchase order follow the same route; see [Raising a purchase order in Tally](../finance/purchase-orders.md) for the ordering side.

### Personal deliveries

You may have small personal parcels delivered to the office, within reason: something that fits under your arm, collected the same day. Please do not send furniture, large appliances or perishable shopping to the office, and do not use it as your regular delivery address.

Food delivered for you, such as a lunch order, does not go to reception. Meet the courier at the building's security desk in the lobby.

### Outgoing post and couriers

- **Post.** Stamped letters and small packets go in the outgoing tray in the post and print room on floor 3 in London. The tray is emptied at 16:00 every working day.
- **Couriers.** Email `workplace@quillmoor.example` with what is going, where to and by when; Workplace books the courier. Packages wait at reception to be collected.
- **Company devices.** Laptops, phones and security keys are never sent through the ordinary post or a courier you booked yourself. Ask Workplace, and see [Travelling with company devices](../security/travel-with-devices.md) if you are taking a device with you instead.

### Things we will not accept

Reception will refuse, or hold back for collection by Workplace, anything that is leaking or damaged, gas cylinders, loose lithium batteries, chemicals, and any parcel addressed to someone who does not work here. If you are expecting anything unusual, such as hardware with large batteries or test equipment, tell Workplace before it arrives.

## Paris: what is different

The same rules apply at Maison Lanterne, with these differences.

- **Registering visitors.** Register them in Perch at least 24 hours ahead, as in London. The list goes to the building's ground-floor reception, which issues the visitor badge. Élodie Vasseur, or the host if she is away, collects visitors from the ground floor.
- **Wi-Fi for visitors.** There is no `QM-Guest` network in Paris. Visitors use the building's guest network, whose code is shown at the ground-floor reception and changes every week.
- **Reception hours.** Quillmoor reception in Paris is staffed from 09:00 to 17:30. Outside those hours, the building's ground-floor reception takes in parcels for us and calls the host when a visitor arrives.
- **Large deliveries.** Maison Lanterne has no loading bay. Large deliveries come through the courtyard entrance and up in the larger of the two lifts. Book them with Élodie at least 48 hours ahead; the courtyard is open to delivery vehicles from 08:00 to 11:00 only.
- **Post.** Élodie takes the outgoing post down at 15:30 every working day.

The [Paris office guide](bureau-de-paris.md) covers the rest of the building.
