# Engineering career ladder

Owner: Ottilie Marsh, chief technology officer, with the heads of engineering. Last reviewed: 2026-06-30.

The ladder describes what we expect of engineers at each level, so that you know where you stand and what growth looks like, and so that managers judge everyone against the same yardstick. It is a guide to judgement, not a checklist. Nobody shows every behaviour at their level, and many show some from the level above long before a promotion.

## Levels

| Level | Title | Scope | Typical time at level |
|---|---|---|---|
| E1 | Engineer | Well-defined tasks, with guidance | 1 to 2 years |
| E2 | Engineer | Features end to end, within a team | 2 to 3 years |
| E3 | Senior engineer | A team's systems; leads work across a quarter | Open-ended |
| E4 | Staff engineer | Problems that span several teams; shapes technical direction | Open-ended |
| E5 | Principal engineer | Company-wide technical strategy | Open-ended |

E3 is a career level: nobody is expected to move beyond it. Engineering managers have a parallel track, from M1, engineering manager, to M3, head of an engineering area. Moving between the tracks is a conversation, not a promotion or a demotion, and it keeps the same pay band.

## Dimensions

Every level is assessed on the same four dimensions.

**Technical skill: code, design and operations.**
- E2 writes clear, tested code that follows the [coding standards](coding-standards.md) without prompting.
- E3 designs systems that other people can operate and change, and writes the ADRs for them.
- E4 sets standards that others follow, and knows when to break them.

**Delivery: getting the right things done.**
- E2 breaks a feature into small changes and ships them.
- E3 plans and leads an epic, managing its risk and scope.
- E4 delivers outcomes that cross team boundaries, such as a platform migration.

**Collaboration: reviews, mentoring and communication.**
- E2 gives reviews that improve the code, within the turnaround in the [code review guidelines](code-review.md).
- E3 mentors E1 and E2 engineers, and runs design reviews for the team.
- E4 grows senior engineers, and resolves disagreements between teams.

**Ownership: production, on-call and the long term.**
- E2 joins the on-call rota and handles common pages with the runbooks.
- E3 acts as IC for incidents in their area, and sees PIR actions through.
- E4 removes whole classes of incident, not just their instances.

## Promotions

1. The engineer and their manager agree that the engineer is working at the next level, usually on the strength of six months or more of evidence.
2. The manager writes a promotion case: half a page per dimension, with links to changes, ADRs, incident work and peer feedback.
3. A calibration panel, made of the heads of engineering and one staff engineer from outside the engineer's area, reads every case in the round together, to keep levels consistent across teams.
4. Decisions are made in the [performance review](../people/performance-reviews.md) cycle, and pay changes follow from them.

Promotions to E4 and E5 also need the chief technology officer's approval.

## Using the ladder

- In one-to-ones, pick one dimension a quarter to grow in, and name the evidence you will look for.
- In hiring, each stage in [interviewing engineers](interviewing.md) maps to these dimensions, and every offer states a level.
- If you disagree with your level, talk to your manager first, then to their head of engineering.
