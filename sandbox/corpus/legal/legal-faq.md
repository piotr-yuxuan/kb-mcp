The questions the Legal team hears most often, with short answers and a pointer to the
page that holds the detail. The policies are the authority; if an answer here ever seems to

Clausebook.

## Contracts

**How do I get a contract reviewed?**
Raise a request in Clausebook, choosing the right type and attaching the document in an
editable format. Request types and response targets are in
[Requesting a contract review](contract-requests.md).

**Can I sign this contract myself?**
It depends on the total contract value and on your role, not on your seniority in your
team. The bands and the contracts that need a more senior signatory are in
[Contract signature authority](contract-signature-authority.md). Clausebook routes each
contract to the right signatory, so you cannot get it wrong if you use it.

**I already have an approved purchase order. Does that mean I can sign the contract?**
No. A purchase order approves the spend; signature authority is a separate check. See the
[spend approval matrix](../finance/spend-approval.md) for the first and the signature
policy for the second.

**Where do I find our templates?**
In Clausebook. The [contract template library](template-library.md) lists them and says
which you may use without Legal.

**A supplier has sent us its standard terms and says everyone signs them unchanged. Do we
need to review them?**
Yes. "Everyone signs them" is a sales line, not a reason. Raise a supplier contract
request.

## NDAs

**Do I need an NDA before talking to a prospective partner?**
Only if you will share Confidential information. If so, use our mutual template; the
[NDA guidance](nda.md) explains when you can send it without Legal.

**The counterparty wants to use its own NDA.**
That needs a Legal review. Ours is usually quicker to agree, so offer it first.

## Merchants

**A merchant wants a higher liability cap. What can I offer?**
Usually nothing beyond our standard. The [MSA clause guide](msa-clause-guide.md) sets out
the cap, the narrow exception and who approves it. Do not hint at flexibility before
checking it.

**A merchant's lawyers have sent a mark-up of the whole MSA.**
Raise an MSA deviation request with the mark-up attached, and do not respond to it clause
by clause yourself. Commercial counsel will propose a call.

**A merchant asks for our DPA or our list of sub-processors.**
Both are covered in the [DPA playbook](dpa-playbook.md). Send the link to the public
sub-processor list rather than a copy.

## Personal data

**Someone has asked for a copy of all the data we hold about them. What do I do?**
That is a DSAR. Do not answer it yourself. If it came through Merchant Support, follow
[Forwarding data subject requests](../support/data-subject-requests.md); otherwise forward
it to `privacy@quillmoor.example` the same day. The clock starts when anyone at Quillmoor
receives it.

**Someone wants us to erase their personal data.**
Forward it to `privacy@quillmoor.example` in the same way. The DPO decides whether it is
valid, and the warehouse side is handled as an erasure request.

**I think personal data may have been exposed.**
Report it to Security straight away at `security@quillmoor.example`. Do not wait to be
sure. Security and the DPO decide who needs to be told and when.

## Code and the Quillmoor name

**Can I use this open-source library?**
Check its licence against the [open-source licence policy](open-source-licences.md). The
CI check will tell you too, but it is better to know before you write the code.

**A merchant wants to put our logo on its website.**
The checkout badge is already allowed by the MSA. Anything more needs permission; see
[Using the Quillmoor name and logo](brand-use.md).

## Conduct

**A supplier has offered me tickets to a match. Can I accept?**
Possibly, with your manager's agreement and a register entry. Not if a tender or
negotiation with that supplier is open. See [Gifts and hospitality](gifts-and-hospitality.md).

**Someone has asked me for a payment to speed up a permit for the Paris office.**
Refuse. That is a facilitation payment, which the [anti-bribery policy](anti-bribery.md)
forbids, and report it to the general counsel.

**I am worried about something I have seen at work, but I do not want to be named.**
Use the independent reporting line described in [Whistleblowing](whistleblowing.md). You
can stay anonymous and still answer our follow-up questions.
