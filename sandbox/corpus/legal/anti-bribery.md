## Our position

Quillmoor does not tolerate bribery in any form. We would rather lose a merchant, a
supplier or a licence than win it by paying, offering or accepting a bribe. This holds in
the UK, in France and anywhere else we do business, whatever the local custom, and it holds
for everyone acting for us, not only employees.

A bribe is anything of value, offered, promised, given, requested or accepted, to
influence someone to act improperly in their role, or to reward them for having done so.
It does not need to be money, and it does not need to succeed: offering is enough. Under
UK and French law the company itself can be prosecuted if someone acting for it pays a
bribe and it cannot show that it had adequate procedures to prevent it. This policy, the
training and the controls below are those procedures.

## What is forbidden

- Offering, promising or giving anything of value to obtain or keep business or an
  advantage improperly.
- Requesting or accepting anything of value in return for acting improperly, for example
  favouring a supplier in a tender.
- **Facilitation payments**: small payments to officials to speed up a routine action,
  such as a licence, a permit or a customs clearance. They are bribes, even where they are
  common, and they are forbidden.
- Making a payment through a third party, such as an agent, consultant or reseller, that
  we could not make ourselves.
- Political donations in Quillmoor's name. Charitable donations need the CFO's approval
  and must never be linked to a business decision.

The one exception is a payment demanded under a threat to someone's safety or liberty.
Pay if you must, get to safety, and report it to the general counsel as soon as you can.

## Public officials

Extra care applies to anyone working for a government, a regulator, a public body or a
state-owned company, including staff at our regulators and at the supervisory authority.
Nothing of value may be offered to a public official, at any amount, without Legal's
prior approval. Ordinary refreshments during a meeting are fine.

## Gifts and hospitality

Reasonable gifts and hospitality are part of normal business. The thresholds, the register
and the examples are in [Gifts and hospitality](gifts-and-hospitality.md).

## Third parties

We are responsible for the people who act for us. Before we appoint an agent, reseller,
referral partner or consultant who will deal with merchants, banks or officials on our
behalf:

1. Legal carries out due diligence proportionate to the risk: ownership, reputation, any
   links to officials, and whether the fee is reasonable for the work.
2. The contract includes our anti-bribery clause, an audit right and a right to end the
   contract immediately for breach. The referral partner template in the
   [contract template library](template-library.md) already has them.
3. Payments go only to a business bank account in the partner's own name, in the country
   where it works, against an invoice describing the work.

Warning signs include requests for payment in cash or to a third country, unusually high
commissions, a partner recommended by an official, or a refusal to sign our clause.

## Books and records

Every payment, gift, hospitality and expense must be recorded accurately, with its real
purpose. Mislabelling a payment, even a small one, is a breach of this policy. Finance
checks expenses and supplier payments against these rules as part of its normal reviews.

## Training

Everyone completes anti-bribery training when they join and every year after that. People
in sales, partnerships, procurement and finance complete an additional module. People
tracks completion, and Legal reports it to the audit committee twice a year.

## Raising a concern

If you are asked for a bribe, suspect one, or see something that does not look right, tell
the general counsel at `legal@quillmoor.example`. If you prefer not to, or if the concern
involves senior management, use the channels in [Whistleblowing](whistleblowing.md),
which include an anonymous option. Nobody will suffer for refusing to pay a bribe or for
raising a concern in good faith, even if it costs us business.

Breaching this policy is a disciplinary matter and may lead to dismissal. It may also be a
crime for which individuals can be prosecuted.
