This policy says who may sign a contract on behalf of Quillmoor Ltd. It applies to every
agreement that binds the company: merchant contracts, supplier contracts, partnerships,
leases, settlement agreements, letters of intent and NDAs. It does not say who may approve
spending; that is a separate check, covered by the
[spend approval matrix](../finance/spend-approval.md). A purchase often needs both: the
spend approved in Tally and the contract signed by someone with authority under this page.

## 1. The rule in one table

Authority depends on the **total contract value**, worked out as described in section 2.

| Total contract value | Who signs | Legal review |
|---|---|---|
| Under £50,000 | The head of department that owns the contract | When the template is changed, or there is no template |
| £50,000 to £250,000 | The CFO | When the template is changed, or there is no template |
| Over £250,000 | The chief executive, after Legal's review | Always |

The CFO's band includes both of its ends: a contract worth exactly £50,000 is signed by
the CFO, and so is a contract worth exactly £250,000. Above £250,000, the chief executive
signs only once Legal has reviewed the final text and recorded its review on the
Clausebook request.

The CFO is Idris Kallenberg and the chief executive is Hollis Varga. A more senior
signatory may always sign in place of a more junior one, but never the other way round.

## 2. Working out the total contract value

The total contract value is the whole amount the contract commits either side to pay over
its committed term, excluding VAT. In practice:

- **Count every year of the committed term**, not the annual amount. A three-year supplier
  contract at £30,000 a year is worth £90,000.
- **Count one-off fees** such as set-up, migration or training.
- **Count the first renewal** if the contract renews automatically and we cannot give
  notice to stop it before the renewal starts.
- **For merchant contracts**, use the fees we expect over the initial term, from the
  pricing in the order form and the volumes in the sales forecast. If the forecast is
  uncertain, take the higher figure.
- **For contracts in euros**, convert at Finance's monthly reference rate for the month in
  which the request is raised.
- **For contracts with no price**, such as NDAs and most letters of intent, the value is
  zero and a head of department may sign, subject to section 4.

Never split one arrangement into several contracts to stay under a band. Legal and
Finance treat a set of contracts with the same counterparty for the same purpose, signed
within 12 months, as one contract for this policy. Splitting is a breach of this policy
even when every individual signature looks correct.

### Delegation

A head of department may delegate their authority in writing, in Clausebook, to a named
colleague for a fixed period of up to 4 weeks, for example to cover leave. Delegation
covers only contracts under £50,000, and the delegate may not delegate further. The CFO
and the chief executive may delegate to each other only.

## 4. Contracts that need more than the value suggests

Some contracts carry risks that the value does not capture. Whatever their value, these
need the signatory below, and Legal's review.

| Contract | Signatory | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Anything with uncapped liability on our side | Chief executive | Exposure is not limited by the price |
| Exclusivity, non-compete or most-favoured-customer terms | Chief executive | Restricts how we do business |
| Agreements with the card schemes, the acquiring bank or our settlement bank | Chief executive | Our licence to operate depends on them |
| Property leases and licences to occupy, in London or Paris | CFO and chief executive, both | Long commitments, often with personal guarantees requested |
| Guarantees, security over assets, loans | The board | Reserved to the board by our articles |
| Settlement agreements with employees or former employees | General counsel and head of people, both | Confidential and often time-critical |
| Employment contracts and offer letters | Head of people, on the People templates | Handled entirely by People |
| NDAs that are not on our template | Head of department, after Legal's review | See the [NDA guidance](nda.md) |
| Merchant contracts with a liability cap above the standard | Per the value band, after the general counsel's approval of the cap | See the [MSA clause guide](msa-clause-guide.md) |

## 5. How a contract gets signed

All contracts are signed through Clausebook. Paper signature is the exception, used when a
counterparty or a document type requires it, such as a deed.

1. **Raise the request** in Clausebook, as described in
   [Requesting a contract review](contract-requests.md). State the total contract value
   and how you calculated it.
2. **Spend approval.** If Quillmoor will pay under the contract, Clausebook checks that a
   purchase order exists in Tally and has been approved. If not, it stops the request and
   points you to [Raising a purchase order in Tally](../finance/purchase-orders.md).
3. **Legal review**, where section 1 or section 4 requires it. Legal marks the final
   version as approved; that version is locked.
4. **Routing.** Clausebook works out the signatory from the value and the contract type.
   You cannot pick a lower signatory; you can choose a more senior one.
5. **Counterparty signs first.** We send the locked version for electronic signature to the
   counterparty, then to our signatory. We sign last, so that we never sign a text the
   counterparty might still change.
6. **Filing.** The signed contract is filed automatically, with its renewal and notice dates
   extracted. The requester is the contract's owner from then on and receives the
   reminders.

### Signatures in a hurry

The signatory bands do not bend because a deadline is close. If a counterparty needs a
signature today and the right signatory is unavailable, tell Legal at once. The CFO and the
chief executive keep slots each week for signature requests, and Legal can usually find a
route; what it cannot do is ratify a signature that should not have happened.

## 7. If something goes wrong

A contract signed by someone without authority may still bind Quillmoor, because the
counterparty is entitled to assume that our people act with authority. That is why the
consequences fall on the person who signed, not on the counterparty.

If you realise that you, or someone in your team, signed without authority:

1. Tell Legal the same day at `legal@quillmoor.example`, with the contract attached.
2. Do not contact the counterparty about it.
3. Legal and the right signatory decide whether to ratify the contract or to renegotiate
   it.

Signing without authority is a disciplinary matter. A genuine mistake, reported promptly,
is treated as such.

## 8. Frequently asked questions

**I am a head of department and the contract is worth £45,000, but it is on the
counterparty's paper. Can I sign?**
Yes, once Legal has reviewed it. The value decides who signs; the template decides
whether Legal reviews it first.

**Does a free trial need a signature?**
If clicking "accept" binds Quillmoor to terms, yes. A free trial with no payment is worth
zero, so a head of department may accept it, but the terms still go through Legal if they
include data processing or intellectual property clauses.

**Our supplier sends its renewal by email and says it renews unless we object. Who
decides?**
The contract's owner, within the same band as the original contract. If the renewal
raises the price above the original band, it moves up a band.

**Can I sign an order form under an existing contract?**
Yes, if the order form's value falls within your band. Its value is counted on its own,
unless it is one of several order forms for the same purpose within 12 months, in which
case they are added together.

**Who signs a contract that both buys and sells?**
Add both directions together. Partnership contracts with revenue share are the usual case,
and Legal will help you estimate them.

**Do these rules apply to our French operations?**
Yes. Quillmoor Ltd signs contracts for the Paris office in the same way. The Paris office
manager, Élodie Vasseur, prepares workplace contracts there; the head of department or a
more senior signatory signs.
