Owner: Inès Moreau, support team lead, France. Last reviewed 2026-10-01.

About a fifth of our merchants run their business in French, most of them in France and Belgium. They are supported by the Paris team, from Maison Lanterne, in their own language. This page is for everyone in Merchant Support, in London as much as in Paris: how French-speaking merchants' tickets are routed, what to do when one lands with you, and the conventions of our French replies.

## Routing

- A merchant whose account language is French in Merchant Hub has their tickets routed to the `fr-marchands` queue.
- The queue is staffed from 09:00 to 18:00 Paris time on French business days; see [Ticket priorities and SLAs](priorities-and-slas.md) for how its clock runs.
- Three T2 specialists work in Paris and handle French-language escalations, refund approvals and disputes. Strategic merchants who speak French keep their named owner, who may be in either office.

## When a French ticket lands in London

It happens when a merchant's account language is English but they write in French, or when they write to a London agent directly.

1. If the ticket is a P1, answer it at once, in English, and attach the `fr-p1-accuse-reception` macro so that the merchant also gets a French acknowledgement. Then follow the usual P1 route.
2. Otherwise, move it to `fr-marchands` and add an internal note saying why. Moving it does not reset its clock, so do it straight away.
3. If the merchant prefers English, reply in English and leave the account language alone; only the merchant changes it, in their Merchant Hub profile.

Out of Paris hours, P1 tickets reach the support duty agent like any other; see [Support shift patterns](shift-patterns.md).

## Writing French replies

The Paris team writes French replies from scratch or from French macros, which are written in French rather than translated from the English ones. A few rules:

- Address the merchant as *vous*, always, even if they write *tu*.
- Use French typography: a space before `:`, `;`, `?` and `!`, and quotation marks « like this ».
- Write amounts the French way: 2 500,50 € with a space between thousands, a decimal comma and the euro sign after the amount; dates as 8 septembre 2026.
- Close with "Bien cordialement," and your first name.
- Do not paste machine translations of English policy text. If a policy page has no French version, ask Inès or a Paris T2 specialist for wording.

Refunds have their own French guide, written for the Paris team: [Traiter une demande de remboursement (marchands français)](remboursements-marchands.md). It follows the same rules as the English page, with figures and examples in euros.

## What differs for French merchants

Very little. The policies are the same in both languages; where a French page gives a money amount, it gives it in euros. A few practical differences:

- French public holidays close the `fr-marchands` queue; English queues stay open, and the reverse is true on bank holidays in England.
- Merchants in France are often paid in EUR, so payout questions usually involve SEPA rather than GBP transfers; check the right currency's cut-off before you answer.
- French-speaking merchants receive the satisfaction survey in French; see [Customer satisfaction surveys](csat.md).
