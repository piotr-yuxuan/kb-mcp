# Annual budget cycle

- **Owner:** Leontine Barrow, financial planning lead
- **Approver:** Idris Kallenberg, CFO
- **Last reviewed:** 2026-09-30
- **Questions:** `finance@quillmoor.example`, or `#ask-finance` in chat

This page explains how Quillmoor sets its budget for the year, who does what and when,
and how the numbers are updated during the year through reforecasts. It is written for
budget holders, that is anyone who owns a cost centre in the
[cost centre list](cost-centres.md), and for the managers who help them.

If you only need to know whether you may spend money you have already budgeted, read the
[spend approval matrix](spend-approval.md) instead: a budget line is permission to plan,
not permission to buy.

## The fiscal year

Quillmoor's fiscal year runs from 1 April to 31 March. We name a fiscal year after the
two calendar years it touches, so FY2026/27 is the year that began on 1 April 2026, the
year we are in now.

| Fiscal quarter | Months |
|---|---|
| Q1 | April to June |
| Q2 | July to September |
| Q3 | October to December |
| Q4 | January to March |

In Finance, "Q2" always means the fiscal quarter. Some pages elsewhere on the wiki, such
as quarterly product plans, use calendar quarters, so when a date matters, check which
one the author means. A useful rule of thumb: our Q1 is the calendar Q2.

## The cycle in one paragraph

Next year's budget is built while the current year is still running. Financial planning
publishes the planning assumptions in late October, budget holders build their drafts in
November and December, and **draft budgets are due on 15 January**. The finance director
and the CFO challenge every draft in late January and early February, the executive team
agrees a consolidated budget at the end of February, and **the board approves it in
March**. On 1 April the approved budget is loaded into the accounting system and into
Tally. During the year the budget itself does not move; instead we publish two
**reforecasts, one in the second quarter and one in the third quarter**.

## Timetable

| When | What happens | Who leads |
|---|---|---|
| Last week of October | Planning assumptions for next year published on this page and by email | Financial planning |
| First week of November | Templates open in the planning model; kick-off session for budget holders, recorded for those who cannot attend | Financial planning |
| November | Q3 reforecast of the current year runs alongside (see below) | Budget holders |
| By 12 December | Headcount plan agreed with People, role by role | Budget holders, People |
| 15 January | **Draft budgets due** in the planning model | Budget holders |
| 16 January to mid-February | Challenge sessions, one per directorate | Finance director, CFO |
| Late February | Executive team agrees the consolidated budget and the board pack | Executive team |
| March | **Board approval** at the March board meeting | The board |
| 1 April | Approved budget loaded into the accounting system and Tally | Finance controller |
| First week of May | First budget against actuals pack for the new year, after the April close | Financial planning |

When 15 January falls on a weekend, drafts are due on the Friday before. We do not move
the deadline later: the challenge sessions are booked in the executives' calendars in
November and there is no slack behind them.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> A draft that is not in the planning model by 15 January is replaced by last year's
> run rate with no growth. The challenge session then starts from that number, and the
> budget holder has to argue every increase from scratch.

## Who does what

| Role | Responsibilities |
|---|---|
| Budget holder | Builds the draft for each cost centre they own; explains the assumptions; owns the variances during the year |
| Financial planning | Publishes assumptions and templates; runs the planning model; consolidates; prepares the board pack |
| People | Provides salary bands, the pay review assumption and the hiring plan's timing |
| Platform and the FinOps working group | Provide the cloud forecast by cost centre (see [cloud cost allocation](cloud-cost-allocation.md)) |
| Finance director | Runs the challenge sessions with the CFO; signs off the consolidated draft before it reaches the executive team |
| CFO | Owns the budget in front of the executive team and the board |
| Executive team | Sets the targets and agrees trade-offs between directorates |
| The board | Approves the budget in March |

Arjun Pelletier, the finance director, and Idris Kallenberg, the CFO, attend every
challenge session. Budget holders may bring one or two people who know the detail, such
as a team lead who owns most of the cloud spend.

## What a draft budget holds

Each cost centre submits twelve monthly columns, April to March, for the following:

1. **Headcount.** Every current employee and every planned role, with its month of
   start. The planning model turns salaries into fully loaded costs with the rates on
   the [headcount planning](headcount-planning.md) page; do not apply your own uplift.
2. **Contractors.** Contractors are not headcount. Budget them as non-headcount costs,
   by month, with an end date.
3. **Cloud.** Taken from the FinOps forecast and adjusted by the budget holder where
   they know something the forecast does not, such as a new pipeline or a migration
   that will retire a cluster.
4. **Software subscriptions.** One line per product, with the renewal month.
5. **Travel and expenses.** Usually a rate per head, with exceptions explained.
6. **Training.** At the rate of the [learning budget](../people/learning-budget.md),
   multiplied by headcount.
7. **Professional fees, events and other costs.** One line each, with a sentence of
   explanation.
8. **One-off projects.** On their own line with a short justification and the month in
   which the spending stops.

Budgets exclude VAT that we can recover. If you are not sure whether a cost carries
recoverable VAT, budget it without VAT and say so in the notes; the finance controller
will correct it.

Every draft also carries a notes tab of no more than one page: what changed against
the current year's forecast and why, and the three biggest risks to the number.

## Planning assumptions

Financial planning publishes one set of assumptions so that every cost centre is built
on the same footing. For reference, these were the assumptions behind the FY2026/27
budget:

| Assumption | FY2026/27 value |
|---|---|
| Planning exchange rate | £1 = €1.17 |
| Supplier price increases | 3% on renewal, unless the contract says otherwise |
| Cloud unit prices | Flat, before commitment discounts |
| Travel and expenses, customer-facing roles | £2,400 per head per year |
| Travel and expenses, other roles | £900 per head per year |
| Vacancy factor | Planned roles start one month after the month planned |

The vacancy factor is applied centrally. Budget holders enter the start month they
really expect, and the model shifts each start by a month when it consolidates, because
experience says roles start later than planned.

The assumptions for next year's budget will be published in the last week of October,
and this table will then be replaced.

## Challenge sessions

Each directorate has one session of 45 minutes. The budget holders present their
drafts against three questions:

- What does this money buy that the business needs next year?
- What would you stop doing if you received 10% less?
- Which numbers are you least sure of, and when will you know more?

Financial planning records the outcome of each session in the planning model: either
the draft is accepted, or it is accepted with named changes, or it comes back with
questions and a new date within a week. Nothing is changed in a draft without the
budget holder knowing it, and every change is visible in the model's history.

## Board approval

In late February the executive team agrees the consolidated budget, and the CFO
presents it to the board at its March meeting. The board pack holds the profit and loss
by month, the headcount plan by directorate, the cash forecast and the main risks. The
FY2026/27 budget was approved at the board meeting of 19 March 2026.

Once the board has approved it, the budget is fixed for the year. It is the baseline
against which we measure every cost centre, every month, until 31 March.

## Reforecasts

The budget does not change during the year, but our view of the year does. We
therefore produce two reforecasts, in the second and third quarters. A reforecast
replaces the forecast; it never replaces the budget.

| | Q2 reforecast | Q3 reforecast |
|---|---|---|
| Opens | First week of August | First week of November |
| Built on | Actuals to the end of July, plus eight months of forecast | Actuals to the end of October, plus five months of forecast |
| Budget holders submit | Within two weeks of opening | Within two weeks of opening |
| CFO sign-off | September | December |
| Shared with | The board, at its September meeting | The board, at its December meeting |

The Q3 reforecast also gives us the run rate from which the next year's budget starts,
which is why it overlaps with the budget kick-off in November.

We do not reforecast in Q1, because there are too few actuals to say anything new, nor
in Q4, because by then everyone is building the next year's budget and the remaining
months are better covered by the monthly accruals described in the
[month-end close calendar](month-end-close.md).

Each month, after the books close, budget holders receive a pack showing actuals
against both the budget and the latest forecast. You are asked to explain any variance
on a cost centre above 5% of the month's budget or £20,000, whichever is larger. A
short sentence in the pack's commentary column is enough.

## Moving money during the year

Budget holders may move budget in the planning model within the limits below. A move
changes the forecast and is reported against the original budget, so the total that the
board approved stays visible.

| Move | Who agrees |
|---|---|
| Between non-headcount lines in one cost centre | The budget holder |
| Between cost centres in one directorate | The finance director |
| Between directorates, or from headcount to non-headcount | The CFO |

Unspent budget does not carry over from one fiscal year to the next. If a purchase
slips from March into April, budget it again in the new year's draft, and tell
financial planning so that the Q3 reforecast shows it moving.

## Worked example: Data Platform's FY2026/27 draft

Tomasz Wilde holds the budget for Data Platform, `CC-3700`. His draft, submitted on
14 January 2026, and the outcome of his challenge session were:

| Line | Draft | Agreed | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Headcount, 14 roles | £1,612,000 | £1,612,000 | One new analytics engineer from July |
| Cloud | £1,140,000 | £1,102,600 | Growth cut from 22% to 18% after the session |
| Software subscriptions | £96,000 | £96,000 | Two renewals in September |
| Contractors | £60,000 | £30,000 | One contractor for three months, not six |
| Travel and expenses | £12,600 | £12,600 | Assumption rate, 14 heads |
| Training | £14,000 | £14,000 | Learning budget rate |
| **Total** | **£2,934,600** | **£2,867,200** | |

In the session the CFO asked what the extra cloud spend bought. The answer was two
new marts for Finance and heavier use of the Conveyor backfill tooling, which justified
most of the growth but not all of it; the remaining 4 points were taken out on the
understanding that the Q2 reforecast would put them back if the new marts' query volume
grew as expected. In the Q2 reforecast it had not, and the forecast stayed at the
agreed figure.

## Frequently asked questions

**My draft will be late. What should I do?**
Tell financial planning before 15 January, not after. If the reason is a decision you
are waiting for, submit the draft with your best assumption and flag it in the notes.

**Can I see other cost centres' budgets?**
You can see the budgets of every cost centre in your directorate. Totals by directorate
are shared with all budget holders after the board approves the budget.

**Does an approved budget mean I can sign a purchase order?**
No. The purchase order still goes through the [spend approval matrix](spend-approval.md),
and any contract through [contract signature authority](../legal/contract-signature-authority.md).
The budget only tells us the money is expected.

**Who decides how much we spend on the cloud?**
You do, for your cost centre, using the FinOps forecast as the starting point. Platform
and Finance review the totals together.

**I have a new team member starting earlier than planned. Do I need to tell anyone?**
Yes: tell financial planning so that the next monthly forecast reflects it. Roles that
were not in the budget at all follow the process on
[headcount planning](headcount-planning.md).
