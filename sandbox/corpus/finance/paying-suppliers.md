# Paying suppliers

**Owner:** Dominic Ferrabee, accounts payable lead. **Last reviewed:** 2026-09-03.

This page is for anyone who buys from a supplier and wants to know when and how the
supplier gets paid, and for suppliers' contacts who ask us that question.

## Our payment terms

Quillmoor pays suppliers on **30-day terms**: an invoice is due 30 days after the date
we receive it, provided it is valid and matches an approved purchase order. We pay on
time, and we expect our suppliers' contracts to say 30 days. If a supplier insists on
shorter terms, the purchase order must say so and the finance director must agree when
approving it.

We do not pay early in exchange for a discount unless the discount is at least 2%, and
then only when accounts payable proposes it.

## What a valid invoice needs

- Our legal name, Quillmoor Ltd, and the supplier's full legal name.
- The **purchase order number**, in the form `PO-` followed by six digits.
- An invoice number and date, and the supplier's VAT number where they are registered.
- Amounts that match the purchase order, with VAT shown separately.
- Bank details that match the ones we hold.

Suppliers send invoices to `finance@quillmoor.example`, one invoice per email, as a PDF
attachment. Invoices sent to individual employees are not received until they reach that
address, so the 30 days do not start until then. If one reaches you, forward it the same
day.

## How an invoice gets paid

1. Accounts payable registers the invoice in Tally, usually on the day it arrives.
2. Tally matches it to the purchase order and to the goods-received record. If all three
   agree, the invoice is approved for payment automatically.
3. If they do not agree, Tally sends the invoice to the person who raised the purchase
   order. Please answer within three business days: confirm receipt in Tally, or tell
   accounts payable what is wrong.
4. Approved invoices are paid in the last **payment run on or before their due date**.
   Payment runs are on Tuesdays and Fridays.
5. Payments in pounds go by UK bank transfer from our operating account; payments in
   euros go by SEPA credit transfer. Other currencies are paid by international
   transfer and may take two business days more.

"No purchase order, no payment": an invoice that does not quote a purchase order is
held until one exists. The rules for raising one are on
[raising a purchase order in Tally](purchase-orders.md), and the approvals it needs on
the [spend approval matrix](spend-approval.md).

## New suppliers and bank details

A new supplier is set up in Tally by accounts payable only, from the supplier's own
documents. Before the first payment, we confirm the bank account by telephone, calling a
number we found independently, never one taken from the email or invoice.

> [!WARNING]
> A request to change a supplier's bank details is the most common route for invoice
> fraud. We act on such a request only after a call-back to a known contact. If you
> receive one, do not reply or forward it to the supplier: send it to
> `finance@quillmoor.example`, and report it as phishing if anything looks odd.

## Urgent payments

A payment outside the Tuesday and Friday runs needs the finance controller's approval
and a reason that is about the supplier's circumstances, not ours. Our own late
purchase order is not a reason.

## Questions about a payment

Ask in `#ask-finance` with the supplier's name and the invoice number. Accounts payable
can see each invoice's status in Tally: received, waiting for match, approved, or paid
with the payment date.
