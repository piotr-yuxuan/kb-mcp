# Travelling with company devices

Owner: Security. Last reviewed: 2026-07-08.

Most trips need nothing more than common sense and the
[laptop security baseline](laptop-baseline.md), which already encrypts your disk and
locks your screen. A few trips need preparation. Booking itself is covered in
[booking business travel](../workplace/business-travel.md), and working from another
country for a stretch in [working from abroad](../people/working-abroad.md).

## Before you go

- **Within the UK and the EU:** nothing to arrange.
- **Anywhere else:** email security@quillmoor.example at least 10 business days before
  you leave, with the countries and dates. For some destinations Security issues a
  loaner laptop and phone with only what you need for the trip, and asks you to leave
  your usual devices at home.
- **If you hold administrator rights,** say so in the email. Administrators travelling
  outside the UK and the EU have production access paused for the trip unless they are
  on call, in which case Security agrees the arrangements with you.

## On the way

- Keep your laptop, phone and hardware security key in your hand luggage, never in
  checked baggage.
- Keep the security key on your keyring or in a pocket, not in the laptop bag. A thief
  who takes both has a better chance than one who takes one.
- Switch the laptop off, rather than putting it to sleep, before border crossings and
  flights. Disk encryption protects a laptop that is off far better than one that is
  asleep.
- Use a privacy screen on trains and planes, and keep Confidential work for places
  where nobody can read over your shoulder. Take calls about incidents, merchants or
  colleagues somewhere private.

## Networks and charging

- Hotel, airport and café Wi-Fi is fine once the company VPN is connected. It connects
  by itself; if it does not, do not work until it does.
- Your phone's hotspot is usually better than public Wi-Fi.
- Charge from a wall socket with your own charger. Avoid public USB charging points,
  or use the data-blocking adapter Security hands out on request.

## Border inspections

Border officials in some countries may ask to inspect your devices or to have them
unlocked. Comply with lawful requests; you are not expected to argue or to put yourself
at risk. If a device left your sight, or you had to unlock it, tell Security as soon
as you can and do not reconnect it to the VPN. Security will check it and, most likely,
reimage it.

## If something goes wrong

Report a lost or stolen device to security@quillmoor.example straight away, wherever
you are and whatever the time, then report it to the local police and keep the
reference. Security locks and wipes the device remotely.

## Coming back

Return a loaner to Security within 5 business days of your return, without copying
anything off it first. It is wiped before anyone uses it again.
